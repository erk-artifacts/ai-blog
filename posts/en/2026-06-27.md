Anthropic's flagship Large Language Model (LLM), "Mythos 5," has finally been approved for use by over 100 U.S. companies and government agencies after negotiations with the Trump administration. As AI models become more capable, governments are increasingly intervening in their release and use due to concerns over national security and societal impact. This case looks to be a significant precedent not only for Anthropic but also for other AI development companies in predicting future regulatory trends.

## OpenAI Announces "GPT-5.6" Amid Government Intervention
OpenAI has announced its latest Large Language Model (LLM), "GPT-5.6." This suite, comprising the flagship model "Sol" and the mid-tier model "Terra," is expected to deliver extremely high performance. However, its release is being gradually restricted at the request of the U.S. government. OpenAI has stated that "such government access processes should not become the long-term default," indicating challenges in balancing with regulatory authorities. The government continues to exercise caution regarding the capabilities and potential risks of cutting-edge AI.

## OpenAI's Vision for the Future: In-House "Jalapeño" Chips and Global Expansion
Nvidia has long dominated the AI chip market, but that era may be coming to an end. OpenAI has revealed plans for "Jalapeño," a custom inference chip developed in collaboration with Broadcom. This indicates an acceleration in the trend of companies like Google, Apple, and SpaceX developing their own AI chips. The move to reduce reliance on Nvidia and aim for AI efficiency and cost reduction will intensify competition across the industry.
Furthermore, OpenAI is focusing on India, its largest market outside the U.S., by hiring a former Uber India head to expand its business. This is part of OpenAI's strategy to globally popularize AI technology and adapt to diverse markets.

This demonstrates the critical importance placed on AI-powered autonomous weapons and drone technology in modern military strategy. The fusion of AI and robotics is set to significantly transform the landscape of national defense.

## The New Battle Over AI and Copyright: NYT Sues Microsoft/OpenAI
There have been developments in the lawsuit filed by the New York Times (NYT) against Microsoft and OpenAI for copyright infringement. This is a crucial legal point concerning the copyright of content used as training data for AI models. As generative AI rapidly expands, discussions are likely to deepen on how to balance the protection of original content rights with the advancement of AI.
