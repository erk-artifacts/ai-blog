## 1. Companies announce support for a shared way to specify AI training-use preferences in one place

**Category: Policy and regulation**

**Confirmed facts (as stated in the sources)**

- OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft announced support for a shared mechanism that allows content-use preferences for AI training to be specified in one place. ([\[1\] ITmedia AI\+](<https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2611/01/2000002776/>))
- A shared “policy token” has been proposed for inclusion in robots\.txt. ([\[2\] The Verge AI](<https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023763/openai-google-microsoft-ai-scraping-standard-opt-out>))

**Why it matters (editorial perspective)**

A mechanism is being considered that would allow website operators to communicate their preferences on the use of their content for training to multiple AI companies at once. The policy token is a proposal to add a setting to the website configuration file robots\.txt.

**Who and what may be affected**

This concerns website operators, AI companies, and the rights holders of online content.

**Possible implications (speculation)**

If a shared mechanism becomes widespread, it could reduce the burden of configuring preferences separately for each company.

**What remains uncertain**

The information includes a proposal-stage mechanism; no implementation timeline or details of how each company will operate it have been provided.
