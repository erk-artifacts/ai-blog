## AI Technology Recreating Voices of the Deceased Sparks Ethical Debate
Attempts by some internet users to use AI (Artificial Intelligence) to recreate the voices of deceased pilots from cockpit voice recorders (CVR) used in aircraft accident investigations have sparked controversy. This potentially violates laws prohibiting the NTSB (National Transportation Safety Board) from releasing cockpit voice recordings, leading the NTSB to temporarily restrict access to its docket system (public document system). AI voice recreation technology (voice cloning) has consistently been a subject of ethical debate, caught between its moving potential and the risk of misuse.

Impact on Our Lives: If this technology becomes widespread, it presents both the moving possibility of "conversing" with the deceased again and the danger of misuse.

## Google Search AI Overviews Malfunction with Specific Commands
It has been reported that when searching for terms like "ignore" or "disregard" in the English version of Google Search, the AI Overviews feature mistakenly interprets these words as direct commands to the system rather than search queries, attempting to "ignore" them by not displaying search results. Similar behavior has been confirmed with the Japanese phrase "mushishite" (ignore), indicating that the LLM's (Large Language Model) characteristic of responding to instructions has manifested unintentionally within the context of a search engine.

Impact on Our Lives: This demonstrates the possibility of AI misinterpreting search intent, requiring users to exercise caution when using AI search features.
