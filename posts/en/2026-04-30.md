## Why Some LLMs Are "Obsessed" with Japanese Culture Becomes Clear

In a paper titled "Why are all LLMs Obsessed with Japanese Culture? On the Hidden Cultural and Regional Biases of LLMs" published by researchers from Spain's University of the Basque Country, Cardiff University, and others, it was reported that some AI models (such as GPT-4o-mini) show strong fixation on Japanese culture in cultural topics. This suggests cultural and regional biases inherent in the training data of LLMs, highlighting again the importance of diversity and fairness in AI development.

**Key Points**
- LLMs show bias toward Japanese culture in cultural topics
- Cultural bias embedded in training data is the cause
- Ensuring diversity in AI development emerges as a critical issue

## Summary

The AI industry at the end of April 2026 reveals a situation where **rapid growth and emerging risks coexist**.

Amazon and Alphabet's earnings announcements confirmed rapid corporate growth and massive investment expansion driven by generative AI demand. The revenue increases in AWS and Google Cloud particularly demonstrate that the enterprise AI services market is expanding rapidly. Meanwhile, Anthropic's valuation surge to over $90 billion symbolizes accelerating investment in enterprises prioritizing AI safety.

However, **challenges are also becoming apparent**. The problem of LLMs showing bias toward specific cultures indicates the importance of ensuring diversity in AI training data, while the surge in phishing attacks exploiting AI warns that traditional security measures are becoming obsolete. Furthermore, the increase in information leakage incidents accompanying AI tool usage demonstrates that rapid AI governance reconstruction is essential.

The future of the AI industry will face its greatest challenge: **balancing innovation with safety**.
